# 190 — v0.2 is the first version that is published; v0.1 stays unpublished

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Agenda topic:** none (the owner's review of v0.2, the step after [decision 189](189-the-owners-review-of-v0.2.md))
- **Decided by:** the owner ("let's publish 0.2 only and skip 0.1"), on Claude's recommendation.
- **Supersedes in part:** [decision 121](121-format-1-freezes-at-v0.1.md) (when format 1 freezes), [decision 117](117-what-json-covers-at-v0.1.md) (when `opalro_json` 1 freezes), [decision 157](157-v0.2-in-four-milestones.md) ("`main` stays what v0.1 is built from until the owner publishes v0.1") and [decision 187](187-v0.2s-package.md) point 6 (the choice between publishing v0.1 first and letting v0.2 replace it, now made)

## Context

v0.1 was packaged on 2026-11-13: a draft release, "Opalro 0.1.0", with six files, waiting for the owner to publish it. It was never published. While it waited, v0.2 was built: lines and tags, combining, conflicts kept and fixed later, `opalro who`. v0.2 does everything v0.1 does. Two Low findings of the red team are in v0.1's draft and are fixed only in v0.2 ([decision 163](163-red-team-m7-session-2-rulings.md)).

Nobody outside has a v0.1: no project exists that a published v0.1 wrote. The owner's own projects, made with builds from before v0.2, do exist.

## Options

- **A. Publish v0.1 first, then v0.2.** Two releases a few days apart; the first with two known findings; v0.2's notes speak to people who used 0.1, as written.
- **B. Publish v0.2 only.** v0.1's draft is never published. v0.2 is the first version people get, and its pages speak to someone who has never had Opalro.
- **C. Rebuild v0.1 with the two fixes, publish it, then v0.2.** The most work, for a version that would be replaced at once.

## Decision

**B.**

**1. v0.1 is never published.** Its draft release is deleted, so that it cannot be published by mistake, and the pull request that would have recorded its publishing (#36) is closed without being merged. Both are done by the owner, or by Claude when the owner says so: a deleted draft cannot be brought back.

**2. v0.2 is the first version people get, and the promises that were to start with v0.1 start with v0.2.** Format 1 freezes when the owner publishes v0.2, with everything decision 121 lists and what v0.2 added: the `conflicts` feature and the conflict record. From then on every later Opalro opens any project v0.2 wrote and reads everything in it, and anything new on disk arrives as a `feature` line or a later format number. The same for `opalro_json` 1 and decision 117: it freezes with v0.2, as v0.2 prints it.

**3. Opalro goes on reading what builds from before v0.2 wrote,** v0.1's draft among them, as it does today: nothing in the engine changes. `opalro setup --upgrade` stays: it turns the `conflicts` feature on in a project that lacks it, and it is how any later feature will arrive. The feature line stays too: it is the mechanism decision 008 chose for every addition, not a leftover of v0.1.

**4. The pages a release carries speak to someone who has never had Opalro.** The release notes say what Opalro 0.2 does, not what changed since 0.1. No page tells a reader about "a project made with Opalro 0.1", compares with "v0.1", or lists as a known problem that 0.1 cannot open an upgraded project. Numbers that were measured on the code before v0.2's milestones and not measured again say so, with their date, not "v0.1".

**5. The record of v0.1 stays.** `docs/releases/v0.1.0*` are kept, each with a line at its top saying that the release was prepared and not made. What v0.1's own list asks of the owner before the first release that people get still applies, now to v0.2, and moves into v0.2's list. The decision records that speak of v0.1 are not rewritten; this record says what became of it.

**6. The version stays 0.2.0.** A first published version numbered 0.2 is true to what happened, and every page, archive name and test already says it.

## Consequences

- `main`, which holds v0.1's code, goes from there straight to v0.2 when the owner merges `v0.2` into it.
- The storage-format and `--json` pages say that their formats freeze when v0.2 is published. The change that records the freeze once it has happened, which pull request #36 was for v0.1, is prepared again for v0.2.
- v0.2's release notes are written again as a first release's.
- Nothing in the program changes, so nothing is reviewed or attacked again but the pages; the release workflow runs once more on `v0.2` after the pages change, since the archives carry the README and the guide.
- Left for the owner: trying it; deleting v0.1's draft and closing #36 (or saying that Claude should); merging `v0.2` into `main`; the release workflow from `main`; publishing; the website; and whether v0.2 needs papers about its code, as v0.1 has.
