## Summary

Across all of it, not one combine lost an added line, brought back a removed one, mixed two files' lines, put a file under a name neither line gave it, left the folder different from the save (folders included), or failed to come back exactly with `undo` and `redo`; no refusal changed the folder, the line, the packs or `.opalro/tmp`. ## Findings

- **RT19-1 Low (a combine of two lines that parted once is refused as "crossing too many times" when saves are dated out of order; T43; `ancestry.rs` `common_starts`).**
  - **What happens.** `common_starts` counts the walk's candidates and returns `TooMany` when there are more than 16, *before* the exact drop that decision 170 added for saves dated alike or backwards. In a history where sixteen saves W1..W16 are combined into V, Z is saved on V, and both lines part from Z and then each combines every Wi again, the Wi are dated after Z (a clock running ahead). The walk marks every Wi with both lines before the mark "under a common save" reaches them, so it ends with 17 candidates (Z and the Wi), and the combine says "The histories of main and side cross each other too many times for Opalro to combine them". The lines parted once, at Z, and the exact answer is Z alone. With all saves dated alike (as in a history from Git with many saves in one second) the same shape is refused or not depending on the saves' IDs: of three runs with 40 Wi, one was refused (`rt19_starts.rs.txt`, `rt19_wide_history_dated_alike`). Nothing is lost; a combine the person needs is refused, at random in the "dated alike" form.
  - **How to see it.** `lines_that_parted_once_combine_whatever_their_saves_dates` (deterministic: the Wi are dated 100 s after Z).
  - **What I would do.** Apply the limit of 16 to the starting points that remain after the exact drop, not to the walk's candidates; bound the drop's own cost separately (it is one `only_in` per candidate), for example by a larger cap on candidates (say 256) that only a planted history reaches.
  - **How likely.** The shape needs saves that combine a save their line already holds, which Git's `merge` and Opalro's `combine` both refuse to make; a planted history or `git commit-tree` makes it. Realistic histories reach many candidates more slowly.

- **RT19-2 Low (move notes that aren't true still cost a combine time, without a bound but the number of saves; T44 and T11; `moves.rs` `noted`).**
  - **What happens.** A save may hold 100,000 move notes, and `noted` checks every note of every save along first parents from a side back to the starting point, true or not (decoding them and looking both paths up in two folders). Fifty planted saves, each holding the same folder as its parent and 100,000 notes that aren't true of it, take 2.4 MB of packs; `combine` over them took 22 s (debug build), against well under a second without the notes. The time grows with each such save, and again with each inner combine whose side walks them (up to 64). `history` over the same saves took 5.5 s, since every save it lists is decoded with its notes.
  - **How to see it.** `untrue_move_notes_dont_make_a_combine_slow` (asserts the combine takes under 5 s; it takes about 22 s).
  - **What I would do.** Bound the notes a combine examines in all (for example the 100,000 of one save, across every save of the walk), and stop believing notes past that bound, as the 64 MiB bound already does for moves by contents, with the `moves_not_followed` warning. A note whose `from` the parent doesn't hold can be dropped before any other lookup; a save with more notes than files that changed could be passed over whole.
  - **How likely.** Only a planted history; it slows, it doesn't harm.

## Suspicions without proof

None worth naming beyond the judgement below.

**Behaviour as designed, noted for the owner.** A file one line removed can come back under another name with no question: when the other line moved it onto a path the starting point held (after taking that path's file away in an earlier save), decision 168 says the move doesn't count ("a move counts only when the place the file went to held nothing at the starting point"), so the removed file reads as the other line's change to the third file, and the result holds it. `git merge` gives the same files (it doesn't detect a rename onto a path that existed). The table's "one side moved it, the other removed it: a conflict" isn't reached. The fuzz's first run found it (seed 3); later runs excluded that case from the invariant.

## What was tried and held

- **Random histories through the real CLI** (`rt19_fuzz.rs.txt`): five files of 21 unique lines each, every line tagged with its file's ID; 1 to 4 saves per line of edits (each line in its own half of a file), moves (to new paths, into new folders, and onto a path freed in an earlier save, so that the save notes the move), removals and new files. Invariants after each combine that went through: every line added on either side is in the result; no line removed on either side is; every file holds only its own lines; every file's path is where ours or theirs had that file; the folder equals the combined save, files and folders; `undo` gives back the folder, folders and line exactly, and `redo` the result. After each refusal: folder, folders, line, packs and `.opalro/tmp` unchanged, and the words name a conflict or a clash. Then the cross combine (the other line combining the first line's old save) must give the same files, and a criss-cross combine with two starting points after more saves on both lines must hold the same invariants (the undo, redo, folders and cross checks were added for the last 120 seeds). 430 seeds after the first exploratory run; no violation but the designed case above.
- **The moves table by hand** (reading `merge_three`, `followed`, `noted`): chains of notes (A to B to C, A to B and back), a note onto a path whose file left in the same walk, one file noted to two places, two files to one, a move on one line onto a path the other freed or moved away, a move into a folder the other line made a file, crossing moves, moves that flip the executable mark, folders emptied by a move on one side and held empty on the other. Each ended in the right file or a conflict.
- **Text merge**: overlapping and touching edits, insertions at one place, line endings, a missing last line feed, the deadline's coarse comparison; the existing property test covers the rest. Nothing found.
- **Names alike**: whether a group of names alike by case and by Unicode form could hold two names that aren't directly alike (making `unheld_clash` return early with nothing): `fold(nfc(name))` is the same for the whole group, so every pair in it is reported directly.
- **Common starting points**: linear chains of 5 to 200 saves dated alike and dated backwards, and the wide shape with 4 and 17 Wi dated alike: all combined (RT19-1 needed more Wi, or dates out of order).
- **Git's words**: `merge -m x side`, `merge -X ours side` and `merge -s ours side` are explained as "Git merges every line named at once" (an imprecise explanation, but nothing runs); `--no-commit`, `--continue`, `--ff-only`, `--strategy=…` reach `combine` and are refused. Nothing runs that Git wouldn't mean.
- **What a person is shown**: a message ending like `  [combined 0123456789ab]` can pass for the mark when colour is off; already the owner's call (milestone-1 follow-ups, T36/T48), not counted.
- **Not tried for lack of time**: crash points inside a combine's switch (the switch is `goto`'s, attacked before), a 1,024-parent save in `show`, rules in `opalro.toml` changed on one line, and v0.1 reading a project with combined saves.
